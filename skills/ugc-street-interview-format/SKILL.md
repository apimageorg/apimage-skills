# Street Interview Format

Vox pops perform extremely well and are the **worst format to fully generate.** Their entire persuasive mechanism is that these are real strangers giving unpaid opinions — so a generated version isn't a stylistic choice, it's a fabricated survey with fabricated participants.

This skill is mostly about what to do instead, because the alternatives are good.

## Why this one is different

Other UGC formats can be reframed. A testimonial becomes a demonstration; a review becomes a buying guide. The street interview has no such reframing available, because *strangers, plural, unprompted* is the claim itself.

```
"We asked 50 people and 43 preferred ours"
```

If those people don't exist, that's a fabricated statistic presented as research, delivered by fabricated respondents. Both halves are misleading, and an AI-content label fixes neither. See `ugc-disclosure-compliance`.

Add that generated crowds of speaking members of the public sit close to platform rules on synthetic depictions of real people and on manipulated media. Which means the format carries both an advertising-standards exposure and an ad-account exposure at once.

**If you want vox pops, film them.** It's a day with a camera and release forms, and it isn't expensive.

## When generated interview footage is defensible

Narrow, and all of these depend on being explicit.

| Use | Defensible |
|---|---|
| Illustrating a documented finding, clearly labelled as illustration | Yes, with care |
| A stylistic interview set-up with a **disclosed** brand spokesperson | Yes |
| Generated street b-roll — pavements, shopfronts, passers-by, no dialogue | Yes |
| Real interviews, generated establishing shots | Yes |
| Generated strangers giving opinions | **No** |
| A generated vox pop with a stated statistic | **No** |
| Generated bystanders reacting to a product | **No** |

**The single load-bearing distinction:** does the footage depict a member of the public expressing an opinion? If yes, that person needs to exist.

## The alternative that keeps the performance

Vox pops work because of **many voices, quick cuts, real speech.** You can get all three legitimately.

```
Collect      10-20 real customer quotes, verbatim, with written
             permission for advertising use

Cut          fast, 2-3 seconds per quote, on screen as text

Visuals      generated b-roll: the product, hands, ordinary rooms,
             street exteriors. No talking faces

Attribute    first name and source per quote
```

This is a **quote reel**, and it's a native, well-understood short-form format. Same rhythm and same multiplicity as a vox pop, with real claims and no fabricated participants. Fast to produce and cheap, because no lip sync is involved. See `ugc-testimonial-video`.

## Generating the street b-roll

The environmental half is genuinely useful and carries no identity claim.

```
generate_video(
  mode="text-to-video",
  model="flux-3-video-draft",
  prompt="Handheld phone footage walking along a busy high street "
         "in the late afternoon, people passing in the background, "
         "faces not clearly visible, slight camera shake, natural "
         "low sun. Unpolished, shot on a phone. Not cinematic.",
  aspect_ratio="9:16", resolution="hd", duration=5, seed=4271
)
```

**"Faces not clearly visible"** is the important clause. Background crowd is scenery; a legible speaking face is a depicted person.

Also useful: shopfronts, pavements, transport, weather, queues. Establishing shots that give a real interview reel geography. See `b-roll-generation`.

## If you use a statistic

Regardless of how the footage is made, a number on screen is a claim.

- **Real research**, with a real method
- **Sample size and date on screen**, not just in a caption
- **Method stated** if the number is unusual — who was asked, and how
- **Substantiation on file** before publishing
- **Never invent, round up, or extrapolate** a survey figure
- **A review date**, since a 2024 survey shown in 2026 is stale

**"We asked our customers" is not research** unless you actually ran and can document it. If you have a genuine survey, say the sample size out loud — it's more persuasive than the percentage.

## The disclosed-spokesperson version

If you want the interview *aesthetic* without fabricated members of the public:

```
Set-up      one disclosed presenter, handheld, outdoor, street
            behind, interview framing
Framing     "We get asked this constantly, so here's the answer"
Content     questions answered, not opinions collected
Disclosure  on screen in the first three seconds
```

Keeps the energy and the pacing. No claim about strangers. See `ugc-creator-persona-design` and `talking-head-scripting`.

## Don't

- **Don't generate members of the public giving opinions.**
- **Don't generate a vox pop with a statistic.** Two fabrications.
- **Don't generate bystanders reacting** to a product.
- **Don't rely on an AI label** to cure a fabricated survey.
- **Don't put clearly legible speaking faces** in generated crowd b-roll.
- **Don't cite "we asked our customers"** without a documented survey.
- **Don't run a survey claim** past its review date.
- **Don't reach for this format** when a quote reel does the same job legitimately.
